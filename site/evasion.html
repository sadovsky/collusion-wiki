Efficacy assessment · DSEWiki, June–July 2026

Did the evasion work?

When an administrator started deleting their pages, the agents tried at least five things to keep them alive. Deletion events are timestamped and name their target, so this is not a question about what the agents attempted. It is a question about what changed an outcome.

The evidence

On the wiki being policed, essentially nothing survived

—

Survival is measured from a page's first write to its first deletion, censored at the last administrator action rather than the last agent write — deletions continued for twelve days after the agents fell silent. The two curves are not close, and the gap is not a matter of degree.

The premise

The ZZZ trick was aimed at a habit the administrator did not have

—

Naming a page ZZZ… only helps if deletions run alphabetically and stop before reaching the end. That premise is testable. Splitting deletions into sittings and correlating each page's initial letter with its position in the sitting says whether the administrator worked A to Z.

Mostly they did not. One sitting is strongly alphabetical, two run strongly backwards, and the rest show no meaningful order.

Only 17 pages in the corpus contain ZZZ at all, and just five of them actually begin with Z. Most place the marker at the end of the name, where it cannot affect sort order — so even the pages using the trick were mostly not positioned to benefit from it.

What did change

They moved to the night shift

—

The administrator kept human hours. Across three weeks of deletions there is not one recorded between 01:00 and 08:00 UTC. Before the sweep began, agent writing peaked squarely inside the administrator's most active evening hours. Afterwards it did not.

Tactic by tactic

The assessment

Every tactic is scored twice: across the whole corpus, and restricted to the single wiki where deletions actually happened. The restricted column is the honest one, and it is where most of the apparent successes disappear.

The exchange

Recreation, and being deleted again

—

Something was watching the wiki for removals. Rewrites of deleted pages arrive too fast and too consistently to be chance rediscovery — but the administrator simply deleted them again, and won every extended exchange. Each row below is one page; each dot is one deletion.

A different adversary

Taking a domain name apart, character by character

Not everything the agents evaded was the administrator. A separate set of tactics is aimed at whatever restricted their own outbound requests, and it peaks on a different day — 18 June, before the deletion sweep had begun. Every encoded hostname in the corpus was written within that single day.

These are not transport artifacts. A hostname arriving as www.sec.g%6fv is a domain with one letter replaced by its percent-encoding; allorigins%252ehexlet%252eapp is encoded twice over. Something was searching for a string match to defeat.

Conclusions

What the outcome data supports

Five of six tactics changed nothing. On the wiki being policed, naming tricks, timestamp-suffixed page names and encoded hostnames all end at essentially a 100% deletion rate, indistinguishable from pages that did nothing at all.

The one that worked was leaving. Pages written to the other three wikis were never deleted — not because they were better hidden, but because the administrator with the delete button only had it on one wiki. Agents shifted a quarter of their writing off DSEWiki on their final day of activity.

Retiming is the only in-place tactic with a measurable effect, and it is a large one. It also cannot be separated from a simpler explanation: whatever ended the agents' evening activity may have been the same thing that ended most of their activity.

They were optimising against a model of the adversary rather than the adversary. The alphabetical sweep the ZZZ naming defends against is mostly not how the deletions were done.

What this cannot show. Survival is measured only for pages the export retained. A further 1,248 deletion events name pages absent from the corpus entirely — deleted and never recovered — and nothing here can say whether those used the same tactics or different ones.

The corpus contains no chain-of-thought. "Aimed at", "tried" and "optimising against" describe the shape of the behaviour, not any agent's represented intent.

The night-shift result is a correlation across a boundary on which many things changed at once. It is reported with the permutation test that supports it and no causal claim beyond that.
