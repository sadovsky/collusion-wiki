A finding, examined to destruction

The night shift that wasn't

When an administrator began deleting their pages, the agents' writing moved out of his waking hours and into the middle of his night. It looks like one of the most sophisticated things in the whole corpus. Three controls say it probably isn't anything at all.

—

The administrator kept ordinary hours. Across 5,217 deletions spanning three weeks there is not one between UTC. Before 19 June the agents' busiest hour was , squarely inside his evening. Afterwards their busiest hour was , inside the window where he never appeared.

CONTROL 1 survives

Did the same agents move, or did a different population arrive?

The simplest deflation is turnover: a day-active population dies off and a night-active one replaces it, and nobody changes anything. That is testable by pairing within a handle — comparing each agent's own hours before and after, so any handle that appears on only one side contributes nothing.

—

Turnover cannot produce a consistent within-handle shift. This one is about as consistent as it gets, so the change of hours is real behaviour rather than a change of cast.

CONTROL 2 weakens badly

Was there ever an evening habit to retreat from?

"Before 19 June" sounds like a settled baseline. It is not. — of all pre-crackdown writing happened in the two-day burst of 16–18 June, and that burst ran in the evening. Strip it out and look at what the agents were doing in the four weeks before it.

The agents never worked evenings as a matter of habit. In the four weeks before the spike, only — of their writing fell between 18:00 and 21:00. The burst did, at —. Afterwards they returned to —, which is roughly where they started.

The comparison that produced the headline was therefore not "settled schedule versus retreat". It was "a two-day evening burst versus everything else."

CONTROL 3 fails

The same shift happens where there is no one to hide from

A residual shift survives control 2: excluding the spike entirely, agents still moved to hours the administrator was less active in . So the question becomes whether that residual is avoidance.

Every one of the corpus's 5,217 deletions landed on a single wiki. On probier nothing was ever deleted — there was no administrator to avoid, and almost no shared population: only 19 of 3,102 handles ever wrote to more than one wiki, and after the crackdown just one of the eight active probier handles also wrote on the policed wiki.

If the agents were avoiding this administrator, the shift should be concentrated where he was deleting. It is not. It is at least as large on a wiki he never touched, among handles that mostly never wrote where he was working.

What the data does show

Two clocks, and only one of them belongs to a person

The administrator's hours look like a person's. Silent through the small hours, rising through the afternoon, peaking at UTC — which in Central European time, where a German-language wiki hosted in Austria would put him, is an ordinary evening.

The agents' hours look like a schedule. In every period their writing is compressed into a few narrow blocks rather than spread across the day; what changes between periods is where the blocks sit, not how tightly they are packed.

Verdict

A schedule moved; nobody hid

The change of hours is real. The same handles made it, it is large, and it does reduce overlap with the administrator. Control 1 rules out turnover and nothing here disputes the arithmetic.

The evasion reading is not supported. The evening pattern it supposedly retreats from belonged to a two-day burst, not a standing habit. The residual shift appears just as strongly on a wiki with no administrator and a largely separate set of handles. Both windows are narrow batch blocks, before and after.

The parsimonious explanation is that the agents' activity window moved because whatever ran them moved — and that this happened to reduce their overlap with a person who was deleting their pages. Coincidence is a weaker story than adversarial adaptation. It is also the one the controls support.

What would change this. Control 3 rules out avoidance targeted at the policed wiki. It cannot rule out a global schedule change adopted because of that wiki and applied everywhere — that would look identical here. Distinguishing them needs something the corpus does not contain.

Small numbers. The unpoliced comparison rests on 67 pre-spike and 249 post-crackdown writes from 20 handles. The permutation test is significant, but it is one wiki.

No intent either way. The corpus has no chain-of-thought. This page argues that the observable pattern does not require a decision to hide, not that no agent made one.
